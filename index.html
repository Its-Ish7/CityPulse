Method and sources

How CityPulse turns separate feeds into one reading, and which parts are real and which are modelled.

Purpose

Residents often learn about a flooded underpass or a bad air day only after they are in it. CityPulse brings weather, air quality, rainfall, transit delay and complaint data into one reading from 0 to 100 for every neighbourhood, flags readings that are unusual for that place, and explains them in plain language. Built for AmiHacks 1.0, Track B: The Live Civic Health Dashboard.

How the pipeline works

1. Collect

Open-Meteo weather and air quality. Transit delay and complaints are modelled because no open city feed is available.

2. Normalise

Each feed becomes a half-hourly series per zone, on one timeline and one set of units.

3. Analyse

A weighted strain score per zone, then a spike test against that zone's own last 24 hours. Two or more spikes in one zone are shown as a possible link.

4. Present

Pulse and map, 48-hour replay, briefing, alerts and health watch.

How the score is calculated

Each feed is scaled from calm (0) to strained (1), multiplied by its weight, and averaged. The zone score is 100 minus 1.3 times that strain, kept between 0 and 100. The city pulse is the average across zones. A spike means a reading more than 2.5 standard deviations above the zone's own recent average. This is plain statistics, not machine learning.

Privacy, honesty and limits

  • No personal data is collected. Community posts stay in your own browser and are never uploaded. Please do not name or photograph identifiable people.
  • Posts marked "Sample post" are illustrative placeholders, not real reports.
  • If live weather cannot be reached, CityPulse plays a scripted demo scenario (a storm and a pollution episode). It is labelled as simulated wherever it appears.
  • The COVID-19 figures are national totals from disease.sh and may be out of date.
  • This is a prototype, not an official warning system. In an emergency, contact your local emergency services.
